Technical reference

How it fits together

The Buddy fetches the weather, its GitHub page, the time, and stock and coin prices when those pages are on. Everything else comes from the laptop.

odoo.com · GitHub · Google Calendar · Anthropic

Laptop buddy-link, a systemd user service, collects what the laptop knows and sends it on. The Chrome extension Monitor Buddy: Odoo reads odoo.com and leaves the result for buddy-link through buddy_odoo_host.py. The tools you run, such as buddy-say, reach the Buddy directly.
Link → Wi-Fi: UDP port 4747, sealed with the link key. → USB: @MB command lines for settings and tests, and the fallback for STATE and Odoo data when Wi-Fi gets no answer. ← USB: the Buddy's log, with its IP address.
Buddy An ESP32-C6 running src/main.cpp, up to 30 frames a second. A network task fetches over the Buddy's own Wi-Fi; a knock task reads the motion sensor. Settings and Wi-Fi in internal flash, animations on the microSD card.

Packets

A request is nonce (12 bytes) | message sealed with AES-256-GCM | tag (16 bytes), at most 1400 bytes. The nonce is the laptop's clock in milliseconds (8 bytes, big-endian) and 4 random bytes. The answer is sealed under the same nonce with the top bit of its first byte set. It holds ok, the build id (the first 8 bytes of the SHA-256 of the firmware's ELF), a status byte (bit 0: the timer runs; 1: animations on the card; 2: join the meeting; 3: lock the laptop) and the answer data.

Security

The port. The Buddy opens port 4747 once it is on Wi-Fi and holds a link key (64 hex digits, given over USB), and ignores it while the setup hotspot is open.

Replays. The Buddy takes a packet only when its time stamp is later than the last one taken and within 60 s of its own clock. A recorded packet can be played back only within a minute of being sent, and only right after the Buddy restarted.

What the key allows. Every message in the table: fake states and Odoo data, text, animations, screenshots, the health report, and answers that lock the laptop or open a meeting. Firmware also needs the signing key.

Updates

buddy_flash.py sends the last build over the Wi-Fi link into the app slot that is not running.

Signed

The tool signs the image with the private update key, ECDSA P-256 over SHA-256, and opens the update with B, the size and the signature. The Buddy hashes each piece as it writes it. After the last one it checks the signature against UPDATE_KEY, the public half built into the running firmware. A mismatch gets the answer refused and the new slot is dropped. A firmware built without UPDATE_KEY refuses every update.

Resumable

The pieces go one at a time, each answered before the next. When they stop for 10 s, the Buddy pauses the update and keeps it for 10 minutes. The signature is deterministic (RFC 6979), so running the tool again sends the same B, and the Buddy answers with the byte to go on from. The tool itself keeps trying for 10 minutes before it gives up.

Rollback

After a good signature the Buddy restarts into the new slot, which runs on trial. The first packet it accepts on the link keeps it: its log says link: new firmware kept. Until then, any restart, from a crash or a power cut, brings back the previous firmware. The tool reads the build id from H answers for 90 s and ends with "The Buddy runs the new firmware." or "… it is back on the one before." Before it sends anything, it compares build ids and stops if the Buddy already runs this build.

USB, the rescue

A USB flash writes the bootloader, the partition table, a fresh otadata and the firmware into app0, so the Buddy starts that firmware whatever the slots held. LittleFS is left alone: settings and Wi-Fi stay. Use it when the Buddy is off the network, when an update keeps failing, and after a change of LINK_KEY or of the update key. If the firmware crashes before an upload can start, hold BOOT while plugging in the cable: the chip then waits in download mode.

Knocks

A task reads the motion sensor every 3 ms and compares each reading with a slowly moving average; the difference, in g, is the jolt. A knock is a jolt between 0.25 g and 6 g after at least 50 ms without one of 0.08 g. The first knock of a pair needs 150 ms of stillness before it, which typing never gives. The second must come 80 to 700 ms after the first. Jolts under a quarter of the first knock are the Buddy rocking on its stand, and do not count. A third knock of at least 60 % of the first cancels the pair as typing or handling.

The pair counts 250 ms after its last knock, unless a finger touched the screen from 300 ms before the first knock on, a jolt of over 6 g came in the last second, or a shake is under way. Each knock makes the eyes blink. A counted pair puts away a line from the laptop or an animation; with neither showing, it brings status 15. While the laptop is locked, only the putting away still works.
